About
// engineer first

Measuring and modeling human movement

I'm a Research Engineer in the Neuromuscular Biomechanics Laboratory at Stanford, and Scientific Program Manager for the Mobilize and FAIR Centers — two NIH-funded centers advancing the software tools researchers use to study human movement.

Day to day, that means two kinds of work. I develop and support open-source biomechanics software in the OpenSim ecosystem, including OpenCap and AddBiomechanics.. And I use those same tools to answer research questions — most recently in sports, evaluating performance from video as part of the Wu Tsai Human Performance Alliance's Digital Athlete project.

02
Research
// current work first

What I work on

Biomechanics

Computational biomechanics and open-source tools

Developer and maintainer within the OpenSim ecosystem — OpenCap for markerless motion capture from smartphone video, and AddBiomechanics for automated model scaling, inverse kinematics, and inverse dynamics. The goal is to enable large scale biomechanic studies using musculoskeletal modeling tools.

Human performance

Sports biomechanics and performance

Video-based methods for measuring athletic movement in sports like baseball, basketball, and football as part of the Wu Tsai Human Performance Alliance's Digital Athlete project.

Closed loop

Adaptive deep brain stimulation

Real-time algorithms that adjust stimulation to what the brain and body are doing — driven by beta burst durations or by gait kinematics — plus the engineering questions that come with them: ramp rates, tolerability, and stimulation artifact in bidirectional devices. Two patent applications came out of this work.

Gait

Gait initiation and freezing

Why the first step fails in Parkinson's disease. Anticipatory postural adjustments, external versus self-triggered cueing, and mechanical assistance from a powered ankle-foot orthosis as a route to more consistent, better-amplified APAs.

Biomarkers

Objective motor biomarkers

Short, portable tasks that yield reliable metrics of bradykinesia, rigidity, tremor, and disease progression — quantitative digitography, robotic quantification of rigidity, and wearable sensors

Toolbox
// how the work gets done

Methods and instruments

Musculoskeletal modeling

OpenSim, OpenCap, and AddBiomechanics: model scaling, inverse kinematics and dynamics, and markerless capture from video.

Biomechanics

Ground reaction forces, center-of-pressure excursions, surface EMG, robotic manipulanda for rigidity, and powered orthosis testbeds.

Neural recording

Chronic subthalamic and pallidal local field potentials from sensing neurostimulators; beta band dynamics, burst detection, and coherence.

Real-time systems

C# applications against an investigational neurostimulator API, PC-in-the-loop control, benchtop validation, and open-source real-time software.
